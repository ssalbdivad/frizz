A **thread** is one effort, start to finish. It is not a chat tab and not a branch. The agent's session *is* the thread: there is no sidecar document to keep in sync, and starting one writes nothing into your repo.

## Workers

The agent driving a thread is its **worker**: a real Claude Code, Codex or ACP agent process, running as you, with your credentials and your CLI configuration. Your skills, settings and `CLAUDE.md` or `AGENTS.md` apply as they would in a session you started yourself.

A worker keeps going until it reaches something only you can settle — a product call, a fork where guessing wrong is expensive, an irreversible action — and then hands back an answerable [question](/docs/questions) rather than a wall of text.

## They run without the tab

Every worker runs in its own detached background process, independent of the browser and of Frizz itself. Close the tab, quit the browser, stop the server, reboot: your threads are all still there when you come back, and Frizz reconnects to the ones still running rather than replaying them from disk.

While an agent is working, Frizz keeps the machine from idle-sleeping, so an overnight run doesn't stall when the laptop decides nobody is there. Closing the lid or pressing sleep still works.

## Sub-agents

A worker can dispatch **sub-agents** for independent prongs of its task. Frizz binds each one back to its parent, so the fan-out lists under the parent on its board and its card. A parent waiting on them wakes as each one returns, and a queued parent can be [snoozed](/docs/snooze#until-its-sub-agents-return) until all of them are back.

## Names and status lines

Each new thread gets a short name, its [handle](/docs/handles), which never changes once shown. While a thread works, a one-line status says what it is doing: point at a thread's row to see it, or turn on **Settings → Always show status lines** to show it after every working thread's name. It also shows in the thread's own header. Both are written by a quick model on your Claude sign-in, and **Settings → Background summaries** turns them off: threads are then named from your prompt or by their agent, with no status line.

## Scratch directory

Each thread gets a folder of its own, `.frizz/threads/<id>/` in the project, where its agent can keep notes and files. It stays empty unless the agent writes something.

## Thread info

A thread's <kbd>⋯</kbd> menu has **Thread info**: its tokens, turns, model requests, tool calls and cost. The menu also opens the thread [in your editor](/docs/editor), in a [terminal](/docs/terminals), fullscreen, or deletes it.

## Pinning and deleting

Pin a thread to keep it at the very top of the list. Delete a thread you no longer need from its menu, or have Frizz delete done threads you have not touched in a while, under **Settings**. Pinned threads are never deleted.
