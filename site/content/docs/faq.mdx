title: FAQ
description: What leaves your machine, what lands in your repo, and the rest.

<Accordions>

<Accordion title="Does Frizz run its own agent or model?">

No. It drives Claude Code, Codex or an ACP agent under the account you are signed in to on your machine: your subscription, your rate limits, your settings. Its own small jobs — naming a thread, its status line, choosing an effort for auto, reading a schedule from a prompt — are short calls on that same Claude sign-in, and **Settings → Background summaries** turns them all off. Frizz runs its own pinned copy of Claude Code and Codex — the exact build it was tested against — rather than whichever version is on your PATH. Set `FRIZZ_CLAUDE_BIN` or `FRIZZ_CODEX_BIN` to point it at another one.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Does anything leave my machine?">

Nothing from Frizz. There's no account and no telemetry, and the server binds to `127.0.0.1`. Reaching it from another device is something you switch on yourself. The agents talk to their providers, and `gh` talks to GitHub, but Frizz is a local process looking at local files.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What happens if I close the tab?">

Nothing. Each thread's agent runs in its own detached background process, independent of the browser and of Frizz itself — you can stop Frizz entirely and your agents keep working. Relaunch, and it reconnects to the sessions still running.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Does it put junk in my repo?">

Barely. Starting a thread writes no thread file into your repo. All Frizz adds is a `.frizz/` directory holding a scratch folder per thread (empty unless the agent writes something) and a couple of small state files. Everything durable lives outside your checkout — under `~/.frizz/` if you already have one, and otherwise in your platform's data directory (`~/Library/Application Support/Frizz` on macOS, `$XDG_DATA_HOME/frizz` on Linux, LocalAppData on Windows) — so you can delete `.frizz/` and keep every thread and setting. Frizz never touches your `.gitignore`: `.frizz/` ignores itself with a `.gitignore` of its own, so it stays out of `git status`.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Do I have to use worktrees?">

No. Frizz doesn't own your git workflow and won't create branches or worktrees behind your back. Tell your agents what you want in [`FRIZZ.md`](/docs/frizz-md). If they put worktrees in `.frizz/worktrees`, Frizz removes one once it has been idle for 7d and is clean, keeping its branch and anything git can't restore, like a `.env`; **Settings → Remove idle worktrees after** changes the wait or turns it off. If you do run Frizz inside a linked worktree, it isolates that worktree's state from its siblings automatically.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I run it on several repos at once?">

Yes. One Frizz server serves every project on your machine. Run `npx frizz` in any of them and each gets its own board, with its own threads, settings and state; **All projects** shows them together.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I reach it from my phone, or from anywhere?">

Yes. Press <kbd>R</kbd> in the terminal running Frizz and pick a private frizz.sh name (no account), a custom one, a Cloudflare Tunnel, Tailscale, or a proxy of your own. Frizz stays on `127.0.0.1` in every case, and the first visit from each device needs a single-use sign-in link. See [remote access](/docs/remote-access).

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What platforms does it run on?">

macOS, Linux and Windows, in a browser or in VS Code, Cursor and Windsurf.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How is this different from other orchestrator apps?">

Those apps wrap your agents in their own workflow. Frizz doesn't: it's a viewer and a queue over the CLIs you already run, with every piece of orchestration judgment sitting in editable text instead of inside the binary.

</Accordion>

</Accordions>
