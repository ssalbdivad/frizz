---
title: Cloudflare Tunnel
description: Reach the board at your own domain, without opening a port.
---

A tunnel reaches a board from anywhere without opening a port. It needs a domain you control on Cloudflare, and [cloudflared](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/connections/connect-networks/downloads/) on your PATH.

## Create the tunnel once

```bash
cloudflared tunnel login
cloudflared tunnel create my-board
cloudflared tunnel route dns my-board board.example.com
```

`login` opens a browser to pick your zone. `create` writes a credentials JSON under `~/.cloudflared/` and prints the tunnel id. `route dns` adds the CNAME that points your hostname at the tunnel.

## Point the tunnel at the board

Write this to `~/.cloudflared/frizz.yml`, which Frizz looks for by name. Keep it out of `config.yml`, whose catch-all shadows other tunnels:

```yaml title="~/.cloudflared/frizz.yml"
tunnel: my-board
credentials-file: /Users/you/.cloudflared/<tunnel-id>.json

ingress:
  - hostname: board.example.com
    service: http://127.0.0.1:9393
  - service: http_status:404
```

## Let Frizz run it

```text
  press R, choose Cloudflare Tunnel

  Hostname  board.example.com
  Tunnel    my-board
```

Frizz asks which tunnel serves the hostname rather than guessing from its first label — a tunnel is rarely named after its host. Both answers are remembered, and it checks that `cloudflared` is on this machine before accepting them.

Frizz then runs the tunnel beside the board, so the two share a lifetime. A tunnel that outlives its board serves Cloudflare error 1033 to anyone who visits; a board that outlives its tunnel is unreachable with nothing to say why.
